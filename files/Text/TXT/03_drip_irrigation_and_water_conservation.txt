Drip irrigation and in-situ moisture conservation save 40 to 60 percent water while increasing crop productivity by 25 to 30 percent. In orchards (pomegranate, guava, citrus) and row crops (cotton, sugarcane, vegetables), maintain operating pressure between 1.0 to 1.5 kg per square centimeter at the lateral end. Flush the sub-main and lateral lines every fortnight to remove silt. If drippers become clogged with carbonate or calcium deposits, perform acid treatment: inject commercial grade hydrochloric acid (33 percent strength) into the drip system until pH drops to 4.0, let it stand in the pipes for 24 hours, and then flush thoroughly with clean water. Apply organic mulching using dry crop residue, sugarcane trash, or wheat straw at 2 to 3 tons per acre around the plant root zone. Mulching reduces soil evaporation by 40 to 50 percent, moderates soil temperature, suppresses weed growth, and promotes earthworm activity in the root zone.
